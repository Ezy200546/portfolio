01 — About

A student still early in the story

I'm Ezy Ibrahima Cherif, a Computer Science and Engineering student at the Islamic University of Technology in Bangladesh. I like studying, and I genuinely enjoy putting myself in front of problems I'm not sure I can solve yet — it's usually the best way I've found to actually measure what I can do.

I moved to Bangladesh to study at IUT, and coming from Guinea, that meant adjusting to an entirely English-medium academic system while I was still learning to speak English properly. It was a genuinely difficult start. Over time, and with a lot of patience, I found my footing — and I credit that growth to the mercy of Allah, who has carried me through it.

Outside the classroom, I'm sociable and easy to get along with. Football takes up a good chunk of my free time — I play it and I watch it. Right now I'm also spending time exploring cybersecurity, which is a field I'm curious about rather than one I'd claim any expertise in yet.

05 — My Journey

From a difficult beginning to continuous growth

When I first arrived in Bangladesh to start my degree, I couldn't speak English properly. Studying every course in an English-medium university was, honestly, one of the hardest adjustments I've faced — lectures, assignments, and even everyday conversations all required a language I was still learning.

Arrival

Started at IUT with a real language barrier, on top of adjusting to a new country and a new academic system.

Adjustment

Kept showing up to classes, kept asking questions, and slowly built up both my English and my confidence in a technical classroom.

Today

I'm now one of the stronger students in my class — a change I still notice and don't take for granted.

I don't see this as something I achieved purely on my own. I believe this progress has been possible by the mercy of Allah, who has never let me down through any of it. I'm sharing this here because I think it says more about who I am than any list of skills could.
